# Hook Formulas

Adapted from sergebulaev/instagram-skills @ 6a12448 (`references/hook-formulas.md`), sergebulaev/linkedin-skills @ c4447ea (`references/hook-formulas.md`) and sergebulaev/facebook-skills @ f3fb266 (`references/hook-formulas.md`); author Sergey Bulaev, MIT (Instagram IG1-IG10, LinkedIn F1-F20, Facebook FB1-FB10). Condensed to the formulas that carry across platforms and rewritten in AKILI's own words. Upstream's engagement multipliers are one author's measurements and are not reproduced.

A hook is the first line (first slide for a carousel). It has one job: earn the next line. Pick the formula by the goal the post should earn, then by the material the user actually has.

## Pick a formula

1. Name the goal: saves, shares, comments, follows or reactions.
2. Check the material: a formula that needs a number or a dated moment is unusable when the brand voice, brief and user message hold neither. Ask, or choose another formula.
3. Check the container (single post, carousel, video brief) against the platform file; the fold position differs per platform.

| Goal | Earned by | Formulas |
|---|---|---|
| Saves | Lists, frameworks, how-tos, repeatable methods | H1, H5, H8, H9, H10 |
| Shares | Contrarian takes, myth-busters, quotable lines, useful tips | H2, H6, H7, H9, H11 |
| Comments | Relatable moments, confessions, easy questions, binary choices | H3, H4, H12, H13 |
| Follows | Proof of change, stories with a turn, a person worth knowing | H4, H6, H8, H14 |
| Reactions | Warmth, behind-the-scenes, gratitude | H3, H14, H15 |

## The formulas

`{...}` marks material the user must supply. Never fill a slot with an invented value.

| ID | Name | Skeleton | Why it works | Do not use when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Number-first result | `{Specific result} in {timeframe}. Here is the exact thing that changed.` | A non-round figure with a referent reads as measurement and promises a method | The figure is not the user's |
| H2 | Contrarian truth | `{Common belief} is wrong. {Plain reason, with one fact.}` | Sharing it lets the reader signal their own view | The author would not defend it in person |
| H3 | Relatable cold-open | `{Small, specific moment the reader has lived.}` then the point | Recognition starts the conversation | The moment is generic ("we have all been there") |
| H4 | Dated confession | `On {date} I {thing that went wrong}. {What it cost.}` | A flat, dated fact carries candor without announcing it | There is no real cost to state |
| H5 | Numbered promise | `{N} {things} that {outcome} (most people miss #{k}).` | Slide or line one is a promise with a loop | The list is padding to reach N |
| H6 | Before / after | `{Before state, with number} -> {after state, with number}. Steps below.` | The gap pulls the reader forward | The numbers cannot be sourced |
| H7 | Myth and truth | `Myth: {belief}. Truth: {correction}.` repeated per slide or paragraph | Each pair is send-worthy | Nobody actually holds the myth |
| H8 | Steal-this framework | `The {name} framework for {outcome}: {step list}.` | The summary is the saved artifact | The framework has no real use yet |
| H9 | Pattern interrupt | `{Unexpected visible or spoken first beat}` then the payoff | Stops the scroll in video and image-led posts | Used on text-only posts |
| H10 | How-I teardown | `How I {result} in {time}: {steps}.` | First-person proof beats generic instruction | No narrator exists (pure framework) |
| H11 | Tiny number | `{One number} {what it measures}.` | Short, screenshot-ready, shareable | The number needs a paragraph of context |
| H12 | Ask-the-reader | `{Specific question about a shared experience?}` | Easy to answer in one line | The question is generic ("thoughts?") |
| H13 | This or that | `{A} or {B}? {One-line stake.}` | Binary choice lowers the cost of replying | Neither side matters to the audience |
| H14 | Named gratitude or spotlight | `{Person} {did what}. {What it changed.}` | Named people amplify it | The person has not agreed to be named |
| H15 | Behind the scenes | `{Real moment from the work, with a detail only an insider has}` | Shows the people behind the account | The detail is staged |

## Density rule

Experienced writers use contrasts, triples and questions all the time; what reads as machine-made is autopilot density plus missing specifics.

- One contrast per post ("not X, it's Y" counts). A second is the tell.
- One triple per post, and its three items are sourced facts or named acts, never adjectives.
- Zero reveal bridges ("The result?", "Plot twist:"). Write the reveal as a plain sentence containing a fact.
- Do not open with a question unless the platform file endorses it; a question near the end tends to land better than one at line one.
- Every abstract line pays for itself with a specific (date, amount, name) within two lines.

## Micro-rules

- Number first, "How I" before "How to", and a real failure inside the first lines beat polished framing. Hold each as a heuristic, not a law.
- Lead a story with the tension, not the context.
- Do not blend two formulas in one hook.
- A hook promises something the post then delivers. A formula whose promise the draft cannot keep (a framework with no steps, a reveal with no fact) is removed.

## Never

- Open with a scene-setter ("In today's fast-paced world") or a throat-clear ("Let's talk about...").
- Pad a one-line idea to fill a long format.
- Use engagement bait as the hook.
- Announce honesty ("let me be honest") with no dated fact behind it.
- Promise a payoff the post does not give.
